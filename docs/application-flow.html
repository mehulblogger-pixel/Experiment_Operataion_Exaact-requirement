Two ways in

Not every job is sold. Read the spine below knowing that stages 1 to 6 apply to work you had to win — and that a second, shorter route exists for work that is already contracted.

Entry 1 · quotation-led

Somebody asked for a price

Runs the full spine, stage 1 through stage 11. The commercial terms are agreed, approved and recorded before any engineer is booked, and the inspection call inherits every one of them.

Enters at stage 1 · traced in the first ledger below

Entry 2 · direct call

The client just instructs the work

A running ARC, a standing rate contract, or an urgent inspection phoned in by a client you already have. No inquiry, no quotation, no approval, no follow-ups, no handover packet.

Enters at stage 7 · skips stages 1–6 · detailed below

The spine

Twelve stages. Stage 5 is the only one that forks — everything before it is a straight line, and everything after it happens only if the quotation was won.

00

Foundations

Super Admin · once, then rarely

Before any inquiry can be typed, four things must exist. This is the stage people skip, and it is the reason a call later cannot be allocated or an invoice cannot be raised against an office that was never created.

Organisation

Offices nested head office → region → branch → site office, each with a head. Every person carries a home office, a role and a reporting manager. Load the whole team from one spreadsheet if it is a first setup.

Masters

62 lists, including SBU, type of inspection, product category, charge units, payment terms, deliverables, expense headings, travel modes and lost reasons. Rename any of them and every screen follows.

People who do the work

Inspection engineers with skills, trade, certifications, weekly working days (5 / 5.5 / 6) and roll type — OWN if we pay the salary, AGENCY if an agency bills us.

Commercial rules

Approval rules by quotation amount band and SBU, the quotation Word template, e-mail templates, default terms & conditions, and per-office overhead and contingency percentages.

What breaks without it

No approval rule means a quotation can be sent without anyone senior seeing it. No office head means escalations fall back to whoever the system can find. The Organisation → Gaps tab names every one of these before it bites.

01

Inquiry

Marketing Executive · BDM · KAM

Somebody asks for a price. Log it before you know whether it is real — the funnel is only honest if the losses are in it too.

Number

INQ-00117 — issued automatically.

Source

EMAIL PHONE REFERRAL WEBSITE EXISTING TENDER OTHER

Status

Opens as OPEN. Becomes QUOTED the moment a quotation against it is accepted, or DROPPED if you never quote.

Also captured

Client, subject, SBU, the person asking and how to reach them.

Branch here

An inquiry can die without a quotation — mark it DROPPED. It stays in the register and in the conversion numbers, which is the point.

02

Build the quotation

Marketing Executive · BDM · KAM

Created from the inquiry, so the client and contact carry across. Choosing the client locks it and fills the contact — a quotation cannot end up addressed to one company and priced for another.

Number and revision

Q-00042 at revision 00. The number never changes; the revision does.

Where the work happens

Any number of sites per quotation, each typed SITE WORKS REGISTERED CORPORATE PLANT WAREHOUSE. The site flows down to the line items.

Each line item

SBU → activity, type of inspection and its sub-types, description, site, order type, quantity, charge unit, rate, amount, deliverables, notes.

Order type

OPEN — an ARC or call-off that will produce many calls over a period. LINE — a specific line-item order.

Commercials

One of 17 payment terms, advance %, validity in days, GST %, currency, and the standard terms & conditions pulled from Settings so nobody retypes them.

Origin

OWN CLIENT_PORTAL TENDER_PORTAL EMAIL_ONLY OTHER — because not every quotation is one we generate. See outcome F.

Attachments

Up to 8 MB per file — scope documents, drawings, the client's enquiry, an approval mail.

Executing offices

More than one may be named, which is what later decides whether money moves between offices as a credit.

State on save

DRAFT. Nothing has left the building and anyone with edit rights can still change it.

03

Approval

Marketing Manager · SBU Head · Business Director

Submitting builds the approval chain from the rules — an amount band, optionally narrowed to one SBU — and mails each approver. The quotation screen says out loud who it is sitting with, so nobody has to ask.

On submit

PENDING_APPROVAL, submission time-stamped, any previous rejection cleared.

Approver approves

APPROVED — ready to send. Requires crm.quote.approve.

Approver rejects

REJECTED with a written comment, plus who rejected it and when. This is outcome C — it loops back, it is not an ending.

Multi-step chains

A rule may stack steps — a big value in one SBU can need the Marketing Manager and then the Business Director. Each step is notified in turn.

04

Send it

BDM · KAM · Marketing Manager

Two ways out, and the choice matters to the client's eye.

From the system

Sent over the configured mailbox with the covering note from the e-mail template.

From your own mailbox

The system hands you a real draft, already carrying the quotation PDF and any attachment marked to go with it. It opens in Outlook with a working Send button, so the client receives it from vishal.shah@… — not from a robot — and the reply lands in that person's inbox.

What the system does the moment it is marked sent

Status SENT, send time stamped, and five follow-ups are scheduled automatically from the send date: D3 at +3 days, D6 at +6, D9 at +9, FORTNIGHT at +15 and MONTH at +30. All five open as PENDING. Editable — a client who asked for two weeks does not get chased on day three.

Then they chase themselves

On its due date each follow-up sends the reminder from the follow-up e-mail template and marks itself SENT. If the quotation has meanwhile been won, lost or expired, the follow-up marks itself SKIPPED instead of mailing a client who has already decided. A person who makes the call by phone marks it DONE with a note.

05

The fork — six things can now happen

The client decides; you record it

This is the whole point of the register. A quotation that is sent has exactly six ends, and five of them are not "won". Each one leaves a different trail.

A

Accepted, no revision

Client says yes to what you sent. Acceptance date recorded.

Follow-ups: every remaining PENDING follow-up flips to SKIPPED — nobody chases a client who has already ordered. The originating inquiry moves to QUOTED.

SENT→ACCEPTED
B

Accepted after revision

Client wants a change — scope, rate, terms. Revising creates revision 01 as a fresh DRAFT under the same number, carrying every field and line item across. The old revision is demoted, a dated snapshot of it is kept with your one-line reason, and the register shows only the current one.

The new revision goes back through approval if the rules still call for it — and a price increase can push it into a higher band and add an approver.

Q-00042 R00→R01 DRAFT →SENT→ACCEPTED
C

Sent back by the approver

Not an ending — a loop. The rejection comment stays on the quotation with the approver's name and the date, so the fix is against something written down rather than remembered.

Correct it and resubmit. The chain is rebuilt from scratch and the old rejection is cleared.

PENDING_APPROVAL→REJECTED →PENDING_APPROVAL
D

Lost after the follow-ups

You chase on day 3, day 6 and day 9. On the third call the client says it went elsewhere. Mark it lost and pick a reason from twelve — awarded to a competitor, price too high, budget withdrawn, project cancelled, no response, scope mismatch, timeline, credit terms, accreditation, handled in-house, duplicate, other.

The two follow-ups still ahead of you flip to SKIPPED. The reason is what makes the lost-analysis report worth reading.

D3 ✓→D6 ✓→ D9 ✓→LOST
E

Expired

All five follow-ups ran, the validity period lapsed, and the client never said yes or no. Distinct from LOST on purpose: nobody beat you, the enquiry simply went cold.

Keeping the two apart is what stops a quiet market looking like a losing sales team.

SENT→ validity lapses →EXPIRED
F

Never left as our document

Some work is quoted straight into the client's procurement portal or a tender site. There is no PDF of ours to send, but the value, the client and the outcome still belong in the register.

Set the origin to CLIENT_PORTAL, TENDER_PORTAL or EMAIL_ONLY. Follow-ups, approval and every outcome above still apply — only the sending step is different.

origin ≠ OWN→ACCEPTED orLOST
Locking

Once a quotation reaches ACCEPTED or LOST it locks — the commercial record behind a live job cannot be quietly edited. A Super Admin can unlock it, and the unlock is recorded.

Change log

Every status move, every revision and every follow-up is stamped with who and when. The final accepted copy is kept as sent, so an argument about what was agreed is settled by the document, not by memory.

06

Handover to operations

Accounts / back-office

Only reachable from ACCEPTED. This is the seam where sales stops and operations starts, and it is a deliberate stop rather than an automatic slide.

Register the client

A prospect becomes a real client record with a code like AHM-26-KAVER-00031 — office, year, short name, sequence. An existing client is matched, not duplicated.

Contract number — settled from either end

The client's own reference, held against the client with the value, the start and end dates and optionally the quantity sold. It is what the inspection call quotes back to them.

Register it here and the contract appears on the client's Contract Numbers tab naming this quotation. Or record the contract on that tab and pick the order from Against quotation — a list of exactly those quotations for this client that still have no number — and the number is written back onto the quotation. Whichever end you start from, both sides end up saying the same thing.

Purchase order

Uploaded here and carried forward — the engineer standing at the works can see the PO that authorises the visit.

Float the packet

The operations team is mailed the handover: scope, sites, types of inspection, deliverables, commercials, contacts. Where mail is not configured yet, it is logged instead of silently lost, and can be re-sent.

07

Inspection call

Coordinator · Operation Manager

The client instructs a specific inspection. Raised against the accepted quotation, so the commercial terms are inherited rather than retyped — retyping is exactly how a call ends up billed differently from what was sold.

Cascades from the quotation

Contract number, the types of inspection sold, product category, value and the basis it is charged on.

Who is involved

Client → vendor / manufacturer → sub-vendor, each with its own contact and site.

Which offices

The contracting office holds the client relationship; the executing office does the work. They are frequently not the same, and every office is both, depending on the contract.

When

Up to five dates, or a weekly pattern — every Tuesday and Thursday until an end date — expanded into real dates you can then edit.

Reporting rhythm

DAILY ALTERNATE WEEKLY FORTNIGHTLY MONTHLY CUSTOM (every N days) NOREPORT.

Documents

A clickable link to the shared drive folder, so the engineer is not hunting for drawings.

Region

Set only by an SBU Head or Business Director — it drives regional reporting and should not drift.

Lead time

Computed from the call date to the first inspection date. The register highlights the ones running late, which is the number a coordinator is actually judged on.

Inter-office credit, stated in words

When the contracting and executing offices differ, the call says plainly: Ahmedabad holds this contract and Pune will do the work, so Ahmedabad gives Pune a credit. You enter what the executing office is to receive; they can come back with the figure they need. When one office does both, it says so and there is no credit at all — only the value billable to the client.

08

Allocate the engineer

Coordinator · Operation Manager

The call becomes a job — a day-based inspection or a resident site posting. Everything set on the call flows through; the only genuinely new decision is who goes.

Job number and type

JOB-00874, typed INSPECTION (day-based) or DEPUTATION (resident posting).

Who can go

Six cards answer it at a glance — total, available, on job, on leave, in office, training. Unassigned people are only listed if they are free today and tomorrow, because a name free for one day is not an allocation.

A date you did not plan for

Check availability on a specific date and see for how long each person is free from it — the answer to "the client wants someone on the 3rd, for four days".

Ours, or not ours

An own-roll engineer, a freelancer, or a sub-contractor's engineer. The choice changes what it costs us and what we can commit to.

Limits that hold

8.5 chargeable hours a day, and each person's own working week of 5, 5.5 or 6 days.

Dates

Up to twenty inspection dates on one job — a month of intermittent witness points does not need twenty jobs.

Deliverables

Chosen here and carried to the engineer, so what they are expected to produce is on the job, not in an e-mail.

Money on the job

Expected credit, credit type — MANDAY MANMONTH LUMP LATER — and direction GIVEN or RECEIVED. Linked to a BOSS number for profitability.

On save

Stage ALLOCATED and the assignment e-mail goes to the engineer with the site, dates, contacts, deliverables and the folder link. Stages then run TRAVELLING → IN_PROGRESS → REPORT_PENDING → SUBMITTED → CLOSED, with ON_HOLD and CANCELLED available at any point.

Nobody is free

The gap becomes a requisition, which runs its own track: OPEN → PROPOSED → OFFERED → HIRED. That is why hiring sits inside this system rather than beside it.

09

Execution and the report

Inspection engineer · reviewing manager

The engineer attends on the inspection dates and produces documents against the deliverables chosen at allocation. Reports are built from a template for that report type and carry a serialised release number.

Report lifecycle

DRAFT → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED → ISSUED, with REJECTED sending it back to the engineer and ARCHIVED at the end of its life.

Inspection result

ACCEPTED ACCEPTED_COND (with observations) REJECTED HOLD NA.

Release decision

RELEASED RELEASED_COND NOT_RELEASED PENDING — separate from the result, because material can pass and still not be released.

What gets produced

Inspection Report, Release Note, NCR, Certificate of Conformity, Daily Progress Report, Final Report, Punch List, Photographic Report, Dimensional Report, Test Certificate Review — whichever the job called for.

Who signs it off

The engineer's mapped approver, or their reporting manager, or a named person, or anyone holding a role — whichever the rule says. Signature and time stamp are held with the document.

Evidence

Photographs and files attached to the report, an endorsement track for the manufacturer's own documents, and an audit log of every action taken on the document.

A finding is not a failure of process

An NCR raised, tracked and closed is the system working. It attaches to the report, and an unclosed one is visible before the job can be treated as finished.

10

Close the job

Inspection engineer · Coordinator

Closure is a single form that will not let you through without the report, and it captures the money spent at the same moment — which is the only moment anyone actually remembers it.

Required

The report upload date. Refused without one unless the call was set to NOREPORT.

Expenses, at closure

Travel, local conveyance, food, lodging, misc, plus any extra headings you have configured. Per-km or actual bill, depending on the heading.

Turnaround

Computed as the days between the last inspection date and the report date, and reported on every dashboard. This is the number clients judge you by.

Sign-off

The report is routed to the engineer's reporting manager as PENDING, and becomes APPROVED or is sent back.

On close

Job marked closed and time-stamped, the closure e-mail goes out, and the job appears in the money queues in Stage 11. Vouchers raised by the engineer settle against these expense lines.

11

Money — two different exits

Finance · Branch Manager

A closed job leaves by one of two doors, and which door depends on a decision taken all the way back at the call: whether one office both held the contract and did the work.

→

Invoice the client

Used when the contracting office is also the executing office, or when the credit has already been given away.

Raise the invoice with number, date, due date and amount; then record the payment when it lands.

closed→unbilled →awaiting payment→ paid

Three queues watch it: Pending — closed but never billed. Awaiting payment — billed, not paid. Overdue — past the due date. Nothing has to be remembered.

→

Inter-office credit

Used when one office held the contract and another did the work. No invoice goes to the client from the executing office — value moves between offices instead.

Direction GIVEN means the contracting office passes value out; RECEIVED means the executing office takes it in. Credit is recorded as raised, then as received.

contracting ≠ executing→expected credit →credit received

A fourth queue watches these: closed jobs where a credit is due to an executing office and has not yet been taken in.

Where it all lands

Everything rolls up against the BOSS number: invoicing done, expenses booked from vouchers and closure, and — for those permitted to see it — the loaded salary cost of the engineers deployed. That is profitability per contract, with renewals chained to the number they continue.

Operations dashboard

Open calls, unallocated work, turnaround, delays.

Finance dashboard

Unbilled, outstanding, overdue, credits pending.

Utilisation

Days sold against days available, by person and by office.

Sales

Pipeline, win rate, lost reasons, follow-up compliance, ageing.

The other way in — a call with no quotation

Everything above assumes the work had to be won. A great deal of inspection work does not: it is instructed under something already agreed. The system expects this — once you choose a client, the quotation box on the call form offers — none / direct call — as a first-class option.

When it happens
  • An ARC or open order already won — every call for the rest of the year draws on it
  • A standing rate contract where prices were agreed once and do not change per job
  • An urgent inspection a known client phones in, to be papered afterwards
  • Work instructed under another office's contract
  • A re-inspection after a non-conformance, against the original order
What you skip
  • Stage 1 — no inquiry
  • Stages 2–3 — no quotation, no approval chain
  • Stage 4 — nothing sent, so no follow-ups are scheduled
  • Stage 5 — no fork: there is nothing to win or lose
  • Stage 6 — no handover packet, because there was no handover
What you cannot skip
  • The client must already exist in the register — the call form will not offer anything until one is chosen
  • An executing office, or nothing can be allocated
  • A value, or the job reaches stage 11 as permanently unbilled
  • The credit direction, if two offices are involved
Identical from stage 7 on
  • Allocation and the availability check
  • Execution, deliverables and the report lifecycle
  • Closure, turnaround and expenses
  • Invoice or inter-office credit — both exits work the same
  • Profitability against the BOSS number
07

Where it joins

Coordinator · Operation Manager

A direct call is the same record as a quotation-led one — same register, same columns, same lead-time and delay highlighting. The single difference is that nothing fills itself in.

This is where the risk sits

On a quotation-led call, the inspection types, product category, SBU, value, charge basis and payment terms all cascade from what was sold, and cannot silently drift. On a direct call the coordinator types every one of them. It is the one place in the system where a job can be scoped differently from what the client actually agreed — and nobody senior sees it before the engineer is booked.

The contract number on a direct call

The box is read-only only while a quotation is driving it. On a direct call it can be typed, so a draw-down against a real ARC stays visible against that contract — which is what you want at year end. Where the ARC exists as a won quotation, still pick it rather than typing: the cascade then brings the rate, the basis and the line item across as well.

What replaces the approval that never happened

Three things, all after the fact rather than before it. The value entered on the call, which drives everything downstream. The register's own lead-time and delay columns. And the profitability roll-up per BOSS number, where a direct call priced wrongly shows up as a thin margin against a contract that should have carried a fat one. If direct calls are common in your business, the honest control is a monthly read of the profitability register, not a hope that the coordinator got it right.

The awkward cases

Every one of these has happened to somebody. Each has a defined place to go rather than an e-mail thread.

The engineer falls ill two days into a four-day inspectionOperations

Put the job ON_HOLD rather than closing it. The days already worked stay on it. Check availability for the remaining dates, allocate a second job to whoever is free, and let both roll up to the same call. Utilisation stays honest for both engineers, and the client sees continuity.

The client cancels after the engineer has already travelledOperations · Finance

The job goes CANCELLED, but the expenses are real and are still captured at closure. The call stays against the quotation with nothing delivered. Whether it is billable is a commercial decision — the numbers to have that conversation are already recorded.

The report is rejected by the reviewing manager after the job was closedReporting

Closure and report sign-off are deliberately separate. The job can be closed with the report uploaded while approval is still PENDING; a rejection sends the document back without reopening the job. Only the document moves.

An accepted quotation turns out to have the wrong rateCommercial

It is locked, and that is intentional — live work depends on it. A Super Admin can unlock it, and the unlock is recorded. Where the client has agreed a change, the cleaner route is a fresh revision, which keeps both the old and new versions on the record.

Two offices disagree on the credit figureFinance

The contracting office enters what it proposes the executing office should receive; the executing office can revert with the figure it needs. Until credit_received is set, the job sits in the pending-credit queue where both branch managers can see it — the disagreement is visible rather than buried.

An open order produces forty calls across a yearCommercial

That is what order type OPEN is for. One quotation, one contract number, one acceptance — and every subsequent call draws down against it. Profitability rolls up to the BOSS number rather than being scattered across forty small jobs, and a renewal chains to the number it continues.

Nobody in the company is free for the dates the client wantsPeople

Three routes, all inside the system. Allocate a freelancer or a sub-contractor's engineer, with the cost recorded differently from an own-roll engineer. Or raise a requisition and run the hiring track. Or go back to the client with the earliest date the availability screen can actually support — which is a better conversation than a missed date.

An invoice goes past its due dateFinance

It moves itself into the overdue queue on the finance dashboard the day it passes the due date. Nothing needs to be remembered, and the queue is scoped — a branch manager sees their own office's overdue invoices, the Business Director sees all of them.

The same client is quoted twice by two different officesCommercial

The client register is company-wide and matched on registration, so the second office finds the existing record rather than creating a duplicate. Both quotations sit against the same client in the register, which is what makes the clash visible before the client points it out.

PART II

The rules the system enforces

14 behaviours that hold whatever anybody types. Each one exists because the alternative was found in real use: a number that meant two things, a day counted twice, an engineer at a gate with nobody to ring. A rule marked blocks refuses the action outright; one marked warns lets the work through and keeps saying so.

A quotation number exists once

The number and its revision are unique across the register. The auto-allotted number steps past anything already taken, so two people saving in the same second cannot collide. An external quotation, where the number is typed by hand, is refused if it clashes.

A contract number identifies one contract

The same number on the same client is a rate contract being drawn down again — allowed, and normal. The same number on a different client is refused, on the accounts screen and on the client master alike.

Where a database already held duplicates from before this rule, the quotation register names them so they can be cleaned rather than silently miscounted.

One submission, one record

Every form carries a one-shot ticket. A double-click, a browser retry, a refresh-and-resend, or the offline queue re-sending an entry the server had already saved, all present a spent ticket and are turned away with “that was already saved.” Reloading the page mints a new ticket, so entering the same thing twice on purpose still works.

A job closes once

Re-posting the closure form says so instead of filing another set of expenses against the same day's work. A line already recorded twice is flagged same as the line above on the deputation, with a Remove button.

A day appears once on a voucher

Adding a date already on the sheet names what is there and its hours. Pulling job days skips any date that already carries an entry — from another job or typed by hand — so a month sheet cannot double-count a day.

A report is filed once for a day

Same type, same inspection, same date is the same report: the one already filed opens instead of a second IRN being burned. Daily reports on different days, and different report types on one day, are unaffected. Endorsements follow the same rule, keyed on document, vendor, heat and drawing.

An expired contract stops scheduling

From the day the end date passes, no work can be allocated against that order. An exception goes straight to the Super Admin — nobody else can allow it, and a Branch Manager is not even shown the decision. Granted for a stated number of allocations, optionally with an expiry of its own.

An exhausted quantity stops scheduling

Once the quantity sold is used up, allocation stops. Here the Branch Manager endorses that the extra work is genuinely needed, and only then does the Super Admin decide. Two people, because over-serving an order is a commercial decision rather than an operational one.

Work does not leave the desk against an empty client record

A client needs a tax identity, an address and a contact with a mobile and an e-mail; a site needs an address and a contact. Quick-add refuses to create less than that. Forwarding a call to an executing office is refused until the gaps are closed — that is the moment somebody has to travel there and somebody has to be invoiced. Only the first forward is gated, so a call already out in the world stays editable.

A contract number is never a reason to stop work

A won order with no number yet shows on the accountant's dashboard as one to raise, and on the coordinator's as outstanding. Calls can still be raised and allocated — the notice simply keeps appearing, on the call register, the call and the deputation, until the number exists.

A contract inside its warning window is expiring, not expired

Within the last 30 days (configurable) everyone concerned is e-mailed once, and the screens say so. Nothing is blocked. That window exists to get the renewal done.

Drawing more than the purchase order has left

Choosing a PO line prices the call from that line's rate and says how much is open. Asking for more warns rather than refuses — an order can be topped up; the point is that it is seen now and not at the invoice.

The value billable is a calculation

Unit rate × quantity, computed on the server so a stale or hand-edited total cannot reach the register. The quantity is how many visit dates the call has: one for a single day, the count for several, and the expanded count for a repeating pattern. A quantity typed by hand is left alone.

A quotation and its contract name each other

Register the number on the order and the contract appears on the client's Contract Numbers tab naming that quotation. Record it on the tab against a quotation — the list offers only those with no number yet — and the number is written back onto the order and the rest of its revision chain.
