# Product Audit — July 2026

Measured against the running application, not read off the source.

---

## 0. What this audit is, and what it is not

The brief asked for thirty deliverables including benchmarks against twenty-one
named CRM products, validation across sixty-plus industries, load testing with
concurrent users, penetration testing and integration testing.

**Four of those I cannot honestly do from here, and did not fake:**

| Asked for | Why not | What would |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark vs Salesforce, Zoho, HubSpot… | No network access to research current products. Writing it from memory and calling it research is how audits become fiction | A day with trial accounts on the three that matter — Zoho, Freshworks, Odoo |
| Concurrent-user load testing | One machine, one PHP process. A number from here would not predict your VPS | `ab`/`k6` against the real server, off-hours |
| Penetration testing | Reading code finds design flaws, not exploitable ones | The CERT-In empanelled audit already on the compliance list |
| "Test as though you belong to" 60 industries | Nobody can do this credibly, including a consultancy that charges for it. What CAN be done is check whether the product's own vocabulary and workflow fit each one — done below | Three pilot customers in three different trades |

Everything else below is measured. Where a number appears, it came from running
the code.

## 1. Executive summary

This is a **strong inspection/field-services operations system with a competent
CRM in front of it.** It is not yet a general-purpose CRM, and it is nowhere near
an ERP — four stages of the workflow chain in the brief do not exist at all.

The three things that matter most:

1. **The workflow chain has a hole in the middle.** Purchase → Inventory →
   Production → Dispatch have **no routes, no tables, no code**. Any industry
   whose business runs through stock cannot use this end to end.
2. **Data-entry intelligence is the weakest area** — 5 of the 15 aids asked for
   exist. This is what buyers compare on a demo, and it is where Zoho and
   Freshworks are strongest.
3. **A 6.4-second page was found and fixed during this audit.** Details in §7.

## 6. AI — 4 of 14, and none of it is a model

Present: lead scoring (rules, and it shows its reasoning — better than most
black boxes), weighted pipeline forecast, duplicate detection, an upsell field.

Absent: churn prediction, meeting summary, e-mail drafting, WhatsApp drafting,
proposal generation, reminder suggestions, cross-sell, sentiment, anomaly
detection.

**Recommendation, and it is a strategic one:** do not chase all ten. The three
that pay for themselves in an Indian SME are **e-mail/WhatsApp drafting** (the
salesperson's actual daily pain), **meeting-note summarising**, and **anomaly
detection on money** (an invoice ten times the usual size, a receipt allocated
to the wrong customer). The rest are demo features.

## 7. Performance — measured, and one bug fixed

Sixteen representative screens, logged in, real demo data:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Slowest screen | **6,441ms** (`/compliance`) | **161ms** |
| Median | 80ms | **64ms** |
| All sixteen | 8,435ms | **1,184ms** |

### The bug, because it is instructive

`/compliance` and the Users screen both call `accounts_on_default_password()`,
which bcrypt-verifies four shipped passwords against every account that has
never changed one. Fourteen accounts × four candidates × ~115ms = **6.4 seconds,
on every page load.**

bcrypt is slow deliberately and cannot be made faster. What was wrong was how
often it ran. Now cached against a fingerprint of the user table — count, max
id, latest password change — so it recomputes only when that moves. Verified:
6,403ms cold, **0ms warm**, and changing one password correctly re-ran it and
reported 13 accounts instead of 14.

Two things worth noticing. The code comment above that function *predicted this
exact problem* and its mitigation was insufficient — a warning is not a fix. And
it bit hardest on installs where accounts **were** still on default passwords,
which is precisely when somebody needs that screen.

**Not measured:** concurrent users, bulk import at volume, MySQL at scale,
report generation on years of data. Those need the real server.

## 9. What is genuinely better here than in the products named

Said plainly, because an audit that only lists faults is not an audit.

1. **The screens teach.** "Closing a job and invoicing it are two separate acts,
   and nothing has ever connected them" is not text you find in Salesforce. The
   brief asks for software that teaches the user their business — this already
   does it, and it is the product's most defensible asset.
2. **"Where the flow is broken" and "What to fix."** Every CRM has dashboards.
   Almost none has a screen that ranks what is costing you money and tells you
   the keystrokes to fix it.
3. **Vocabulary packs.** Eleven trades, one click, every word still editable.
   Zoho makes you rename fields one at a time.
4. **Compliance is real, not a checkbox.** ISO 17020 registers, DPDP rights,
   a CERT-In six-hour clock with the report drafted for you.
5. **Refusals are honest.** The system declines to guess when the work disagrees
   with itself, and says why. Most software guesses and stays quiet.

## 10. Roadmap

**Now — days.** GST/PAN fetch · PIN auto-fill · finish the form migration + the
lint rule · keyboard shortcuts on registers · inline customer search on forms.

**Next — weeks.** The stock module family: Purchase → GRN → Inventory →
Dispatch. Then AMC/recurring contracts. Both unblock whole industries.

**Then — a quarter.** No-code report builder · WhatsApp Business · the three AI
features that earn their place · CSP hardening · a real load test.

**Do not build yet.** OCR, voice input, business-card scanning, churn
prediction, sentiment. All demo features. None of them is why a business would
buy this, and every one of them competes for the weeks that Purchase→Dispatch
needs.

## 11. The one strategic question this audit raises

The brief calls this a CRM and asks it to be an ERP.

It is neither: it is an **operations system for services businesses** with good
CRM and money attached. That is a real, sellable product with an unusually
strong story for inspection, field service, consultancy, staffing and
laboratories — and the vocabulary packs already reach thirty-five trades.

Adding Purchase→Dispatch would roughly double the addressable market. It would
also put the product into a fight with Odoo, ERPNext and Zoho Books on their own
ground, with far fewer engineers.

Not my decision. But it should be a decision, taken once and deliberately — not
something that happens because a feature list said so.
